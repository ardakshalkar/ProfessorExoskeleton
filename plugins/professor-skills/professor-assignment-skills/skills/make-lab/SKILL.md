# Make a lab

Design the lab as **pre-lab -> in-lab -> post-lab**. First choose SKETCH,
STANDARD, or ASSURANCE; an explicit depth wins and a complete lab defaults to
STANDARD. Read the
[activity package contract](references/activity-design.md) before creating a
complete package.

## 1. Set inquiry autonomy

Choose or infer one level:

| Level | Problem | Procedure | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| controlled | given | given | largely known |
| structured | given | given | student discovers |
| guided inquiry | given | student designs | unknown |
| open inquiry | student defines | student designs | unknown |

Increase autonomy, not mere procedural length, when greater challenge is
needed. State the chosen level.

## 2. Design the three stages

- **Pre-lab:** objective, essential theory, preparation check, tools or
  equipment, data, constraints, and hazards.
- **In-lab:** investigation or procedure, checkpoints, observations,
  measurements, debugging, and a recording structure.
- **Post-lab:** analysis, interpretation, uncertainty or limitations,
  conclusions, communication, and reflection.

Safety is mandatory when physical, chemical, biological, electrical, field, or
other credible hazards exist. Use supplied and authoritative safety information;
never invent compliance claims.

## 3. Produce two views

At SKETCH depth, produce only the requested stage, procedure fragment, safety
check, or outline. At STANDARD and ASSURANCE depth, produce two views and an
`activity.yaml` proposal when its required identifiers are known, as defined in
the shared contract.

The student artifact contains objectives, preparation, setup, safety and
constraints, investigation, recording template, analysis prompts, deliverables,
and evaluation criteria.

The professor guide contains setup checks, expected observations or result
ranges, likely misconceptions, failure modes, troubleshooting, intervention
points, sample analysis, timing, and rubric or feedback guidance.

At ASSURANCE depth, make alignment, workload, accessibility, safety, integrity,
and any variant-equivalence checks explicit. Stop at the design for approval
when the professor requested approval first.

## Rules

- Preserve genuine observation and decision-making; do not reveal discoveries
  that students are meant to make.
- Distinguish a learning failure from equipment, environment, or data failure.
- For software labs, treat tests, logs, and intermediate artifacts as evidence;
  for empirical labs, preserve raw observations and anomalies.
- Never claim a setup is safe or validated without evidence.
