# Make a seminar

The main artifact is a discussion architecture, not a slide deck or a list of
ten unrelated questions. First choose SKETCH, STANDARD, or ASSURANCE; an
explicit depth wins and a complete seminar defaults to STANDARD. Read the
[activity package contract](references/activity-design.md) before creating a
complete package.

## 1. Choose the seminar form

Select reading discussion, case, debate, paper critique, student-led seminar,
or another supplied form. Define the intellectual capability students should
practice: explain, compare, identify assumptions, critique evidence, evaluate,
synthesize, or construct a position.

## 2. Build the arc

Use:

```text
preparation -> engaging opening -> exploration segments -> synthesis -> exit
```

Plan a short chain of primary questions. For each important question, add
evidence probes, likely response paths, follow-ups, counterpoints, and a transfer
question. Use fewer, better questions; preserve time for students to think and
respond.

## 3. Produce two views

At SKETCH depth, produce only the requested question chain, preparation task,
discussion segment, or outline. At STANDARD and ASSURANCE depth, produce two
views and an `activity.yaml` proposal when its required identifiers are known,
as defined in the shared contract.

The student preparation sheet includes purpose, readings or case, preparation
questions, evidence to extract, preliminary position, participation expectations,
permitted resources, and a short post-seminar synthesis.

The professor plan includes timings, opening trigger, question sequence,
anticipated response branches, probes, misconceptions, inclusion moves,
transitions, synthesis, and evidence or rubric notes when participation is
assessed.

At ASSURANCE depth, make alignment, workload, accessibility and inclusion,
integrity, and any variant-equivalence checks explicit. Stop at the design for
approval when the professor requested approval first.

## Rules

- Do not turn the seminar into a hidden lecture.
- Do not assess personality, confidence, accent, or speaking speed.
- When grading participation, value preparation, evidence, listening,
  contribution, response to peers, and synthesis through observable criteria.
- Never invent readings, page references, quotations, or source claims.
